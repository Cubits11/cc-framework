# V3 From V1 Critique

## What Works Visually

The V1 hero render has a strong first principle: the viewer immediately sees "Not safety scores. Evidence-bound claims." That thesis text is the clearest and most valuable visual element. The central capsule also works as a recognizable body for the claim, and the existing use of blue-white evidence light, amber review/decay accents, and red expiration/invalidation language already points toward a useful semantic color system.

The best reusable motif is the bounded capsule with evidence sheets inside it. It makes the claim feel like an artifact rather than a slogan. The circular replay floor and the orbiting support graph are also promising motifs, but they need separation into chambers so they can explain themselves without competing for the hero shot.

## What Works Philosophically

V1 does not present safety as a score or badge. It already treats the claim as bounded, receipt-bearing, and caveated. The render includes a Non-Claims Wall, a decay clock, a replay manifest, a support graph, and a human review station, which are exactly the right conceptual primitives for evidence-bound claims.

The most important philosophical win is that the render refuses the easy visual language of "safe AI." It does not use a green shield, green checkmark, or approval stamp. That restraint should survive into V3.

## What Is Too Cluttered

The hero image tries to teach the entire system at once. The support graph, capsule internals, decay clock, Non-Claims Wall, replay manifest floor, and human review station all occupy the same composition. Too many lines compete at similar brightness, especially the graph beams, rings, and wall guide lines.

Small text appears everywhere, and most of it is not readable at hero distance. The result is a credible observatory, but the viewer has to work too hard to know which element is the main lesson.

## What Is Hard To Read

The central capsule has a visible silhouette, but its material stack is too foggy and the internal plates are not separated enough. The viewer can tell there are documents inside, but cannot quickly understand that they are `cc_report.json`, `claim_governance_audit.json`, `claim_envelope.json`, and the capsule manifest schema.

The support graph is present but visually crowded. Edge labels and node labels overlap the capsule and each other. The human review station is visible at the right edge, but it is not compositionally resolved: it reads like a distant UI prop rather than accountable authority.

## What Feels Generic Sci-Fi

The glowing rings and dense holographic overlays can drift toward generic sci-fi control-room language. V1 is strongest when it uses physical metaphors tied to evidence governance: capsule, wall, clock, manifest, receipt, review station. It is weakest when every system becomes a glowing line of equal status.

V3 should keep the cinematic darkness and luminous evidence language, but it should make each chamber feel like a specific governance instrument, not a decorative interface.

## What Lacks Physicality

The Non-Claims Wall should feel more protective and monumental. The decay clock should become a real mechanism with Fresh, Degraded, and Expired zones. The replay manifest should feel like a floor engine, not only text on a disc. The support graph should become an orrery with typed relationships, not a flat network overlay.

The claim capsule itself needs stronger rim geometry, clearer glass boundaries, and better-spaced internal plates. It should feel like the claim has a body and that evidence enters the body only through typed roles.

## Reusable Visual Motifs

- The bounded glass claim capsule.
- Blue-white evidence tablets.
- Pale receipt-integrity edges.
- Amber review and decay markers.
- Red invalidation/failure surfaces.
- The Non-Claims Wall as semantic firewall.
- The replay manifest as a circular floor engine.
- Typed support graph beams as an orrery.
- Finite Frechet atoms as a subtle mathematical field.

## What Should Be Removed Or Demoted

Small labels should be removed from the hero shot or demoted to chamber close-ups. Generic orbiting panels should be reduced unless they clarify the chamber map. Lines that do not carry a specific semantic role should be dimmed or deleted.

The hero shot should not attempt to expose every filename, schema, and relation. Those belong in detail cameras.

## What Should Become Its Own Chamber

- Non-claims need a dedicated wall chamber.
- Decay needs a mechanical clock room.
- Support edges need a typed orrery.
- Replay needs a manifest engine.
- Evidence roles need a vault.
- Challenge cases need a boundary-testing range.
- Human review needs a tribunal station.
- Frechet endpoints need a mathematical garden.

## How V3 Improves It

V3 turns the overloaded V1 hero into a connected modular world. The hero shot will teach the thesis in one second: central claim body, Non-Claims Wall, decay mechanism, replay floor, and thesis text. Detail shots will teach the machinery: evidence roles, typed support, challenge probes, human review, ledger lifecycle, and Frechet endpoint witnesses.

The rule for V3 is:

```text
The hero shot must teach the thesis in one second.
Detail shots can teach the machinery.
```
